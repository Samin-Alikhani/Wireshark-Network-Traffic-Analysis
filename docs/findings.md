# Lab Notes

## What I Worked On

I used Wireshark to examine the traffic generated by `dig`, `ping`, and `curl`
on my Mac. I focused on normal DNS, ICMP, TCP, and TLS exchanges to understand
how the requests and responses fit together.

## DNS

I found the A-record query from `dig example.com` and its response. Both had
transaction ID `0x9226`. I also checked the requested domain, record type, and
class. This gave me a way to match the packets beyond looking at their order
in the packet list.

## ICMP

I counted four Echo requests and four replies for `1.1.1.1`. Their identifiers
and sequence numbers helped me pair them up. All four requests received a
reply during this capture.

## TCP

I followed the HTTPS connection in TCP stream 6 and identified the SYN,
SYN-ACK, and ACK handshake. Looking at the sequence and acknowledgment numbers
helped me understand how each side acknowledged the other.

Later in the stream, the client started closing the connection. I followed
the four-packet FIN/ACK exchange and did not see a reset in that connection.

## TLS

I found `example.com` in the Client Hello's SNI field. The client offered
several TLS versions and listed HTTP/2 and HTTP/1.1 through ALPN. It also
included an X25519 key share.

The Server Hello selected TLS 1.3 and `TLS_CHACHA20_POLY1305_SHA256`, with an
X25519 key share. I checked the `supported_versions` extension to identify
TLS 1.3 because the older version fields still showed TLS 1.2.

After the handshake, I saw TLS Application Data in both directions. One
takeaway was that the client's list of options and the server's selected
settings answer different questions.
